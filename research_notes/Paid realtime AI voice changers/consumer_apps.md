# Paid consumer desktop real-time AI voice changers (as of 14 Nov 2026)

Scope: paid consumer (subscription, credit or lifetime) desktop apps that change your voice live and show up as a virtual microphone in Discord, Telegram Desktop, WhatsApp Desktop and Google Meet. Research date: 2026-11-14.

**Source-quality warning (applies throughout):** Most "best voice changer 2026" pages are written by vendors or are SEO affiliate pages. Biased sources seen in this research:
- dubbingai.io/articles (Dubbing AI ranks itself #1)
- voxbooster.com/blog (dozens of "X vs VoxBooster" and "best X alternative" posts)
- filme.imyfone.com (iMyFone/MagicMic's own "Top 11")
- voicechanger.easeus.com (EaseUS VoiceWave)
- filmora.wondershare.com ("Voicemod review" with "free alternatives")

I found **no TechRadar, PCMag or Tom's Guide review** of these apps for 2025-2026 (search returned none). The closest thing to a multi-tool test is fast.io (updated May 20, 2026). Its publisher doesn't sell a voice changer, but it explains little of its method and reuses Toolworthy's figures. Prices on aggregator sites conflict a lot. Official pages are preferred below.

## 1. Candidates, pricing, and how credits and subscriptions work

### Takeaway
Most apps charge a flat subscription or a lifetime licence with unlimited real-time use: Voicemod, HitPaw VoicePea, VoxBooster, MagicMic, Shift (formerly Supertone Shift), and probably Dubbing AI. The real credit or minute systems are:
- **Altered Studio:** its online voice morphing is metered in minutes (3 / 60 / 180 min per month).
- **Voice.ai:** its public pricing has turned into a credit plan for TTS, voice agents and audio tools ($5–$880/mo). It doesn't say what the desktop real-time changer costs in credits.
- **Shift:** gives "3000 free credits" to unlock voices, then sells per-voice perpetual licences ($149.99/voice).

Cheapest unlimited paid options found on official pages: VoxBooster ($23.99/yr or $40.99 lifetime) and HitPaw VoicePea Standard ($29.95/yr or $49.95 lifetime).

## 2. Local (GPU) vs cloud processing, hardware needs, and latency

### Takeaway
Latency numbers are almost all vendor claims and conflict badly. Dubbing AI alone has published both "<30 ms" and "300 ms." Competitors also publish unflattering estimates of each other: Voice.ai is "~100 ms+" per Dubbing AI and "400–500 ms" per VoxBooster. Hardware needs:
- **Shift:** says "no GPU needed"; its Japanese page claims 47 ms.
- **Altered:** desktop real-time needs a Windows PC with an 8 GB+ NVIDIA GPU.
- **HitPaw:** the Mac app needs internet for real-time transformation, so at least partly cloud.

No independent lab-grade latency benchmark was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Shift:**
  - "Experience high-quality voice conversion on any device, no GPU needed," and "best-in-class latency, as close as possible to real-time" — [antinodeaudio.com/shift](https://antinodeaudio.com/shift)
  - The Japanese page claims **47 ms** latency, "among the shortest in the industry" (vendor claim, no independent benchmark found) — [supertone.ai/ja/shift](https://supertone.ai/ja/shift)
  - Requirements: Windows 10+ (64-bit) and macOS 10.14+; app version v2.1 — [antinodeaudio.com/shift](https://antinodeaudio.com/shift)
- **Altered:**
  - Desktop real-time needs a "Windows PC with 8GB+ Nvidia GPU (GTX 1080, RTX 2070, RTX 3050 or greater)"
  - Local cloning needs an RTX 2080/3080/4080 with 12 GB as a minimum, and an RTX 3090/4090 with 24 GB is recommended
  - The online (cloud) option is metered in minutes
  - — [altered.ai/pricing](https://www.altered.ai/pricing/)
- **Dubbing AI** (vendor claims that contradict each other):
  - "Sub-30ms real-time processing latency," "on-device AI processing," "Requires an internet connection for initial voice loading," and "Ultra-low CPU footprint (2-3%)" — [Dubbing AI vendor article](https://dubbingai.io/articles/en/list/best-real-time-voice-changers-compared)
  - Elsewhere the vendor states "just 300 ms of latency" — [search summary citing dubbingai.io](https://dubbingai.io/articles/en/list/top-real-time-voice-changers)
  - fast.io says "measured latency came in around 30ms" — [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/)
- **Voicemod:**
  - fast.io claims "around 12-20ms on a mid-range PC" and groups Voicemod with Clownfish as local processing — [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/)
  - Dubbing AI's vendor article claims "~50-80ms" and says AI voices are "noticeably laggier" (biased competitor) — [Dubbing AI vendor article](https://dubbingai.io/articles/en/list/best-real-time-voice-changers-compared)
- **Voice.ai** (three conflicting figures):
  - fast.io: "claims sub-50ms latency, which held up in our testing on a desktop with a dedicated GPU" — [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/)
  - Dubbing AI (competitor): "~100ms+" — [Dubbing AI vendor article](https://dubbingai.io/articles/en/list/best-real-time-voice-changers-compared)
  - VoxBooster (competitor): "around 400–500ms under ideal conditions" — [VoxBooster vendor blog](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voxbooster-vs-voice-ai-detailed-2026/)
- **VoxBooster** (self-claim): local inference using WASAPI exclusive mode with buffers "as small as 10ms," and "typical end-to-end latency lands under 300ms on a mid-range PC" — [VoxBooster vendor blog](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voxbooster-vs-voice-ai-detailed-2026/)
- **HitPaw VoicePea:** a Mac download listing says "an internet connection is required for real-time voice transformation," which suggests cloud-side processing, at least on Mac — [FileHorse Mac listing (via search)](https://mac.filehorse.com/download-hitpaw-voice-changer/)
- **Cloud trade-off in general:** cloud processing "often pushing total delay past 150 milliseconds," and latency depends on internet speed (roundup claims) — [VoxBooster blog (biased)](https://voxbooster.com/blog/best-ai-voice-changer-2026/); [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/)
- **Free local alternatives**, for comparison:
  - Beatrice (Windows) claims about 10 ms latency, runs locally, and uses VB-CABLE as a virtual mic — [GitHub Beatrice](https://github.com/Project-Beatrice-V2/Beatrice-voicechanger-windows)
  - W-Okada is described as the "gold standard for open-source, local real-time voice conversion" — [roundup](https://theairankings.com/best-ai-voice-changer/)

### Inferences
- Sub-20 ms figures (fast.io's Voicemod number) almost certainly apply to classic DSP effects, not neural voice conversion. Neural real-time changers usually buffer audio in chunks, so expect roughly 50–300+ ms of added delay in practice. Treat all marketing latency numbers as best-case.
- For a weak PC, the realistic paid options are those claiming "no GPU" or using cloud processing: Shift, Dubbing AI (claims a low CPU footprint), and HitPaw (needs internet). Altered's desktop real-time and the RVC-style local engines need a decent NVIDIA GPU.
- In a call, the app's own delay (Discord, Meet, WhatsApp) adds to the changer's delay, so a 150–300 ms changer can make conversation feel noticeably laggy. This is general reasoning, not a sourced test.

### Gaps
- No independent measured latency, in ms, for Shift, HitPaw, MagicMic, FineVoice, Altered or VoxBooster.
- Voice.ai's hardware requirement: an aggregator mentions a GTX 980 / RX 580 minimum, but I couldn't tie it to an official source. Whether its real-time voices run locally or in the cloud is also unconfirmed.
- MagicMic and FineVoice: local vs cloud processing not documented in the sources found.

## 3. Realism (human-sounding vs robotic), voice libraries, and custom voices / cloning

### Takeaway
No source gives numeric realism scores for live consumer changers. Qualitative reports:
- **Voicemod:** effects "lean toward novelty and gaming rather than realistic transformation." Trustpilot users call some voices "robotic, unrealistic, or laggy."
- **Voice.ai:** described as "noticeably more natural" than preset changers.
- **Altered:** "can sound slightly robotic on longer passages."
- **MagicMic:** quality "varies across different voice presets."
- **Shift:** independent realism evidence is absent.

Real cloning of your own target voice for live use is mainly offered by Altered (rapid and local cloning, GPU-heavy) and Voice.ai (instant clones on paid credit plans). Voicemod's VoiceLab builds effect chains, not neural clones, according to its help text.

- Realism usually suffers when the target voice's gender or pitch differs a lot from yours, or when you have a weak mic. Expect artifacts on breaths and laughs. This is general domain knowledge and wasn't verified in sources.

## 4. Platform support and compatibility with Discord, Telegram Desktop, WhatsApp Desktop and Google Meet

### Takeaway
All candidates work by installing a virtual microphone, or by routing through VB-CABLE for open-source tools. You pick that device as the input in the calling app, so they work in any app that lets you choose the mic. Discord is the documented primary target everywhere. WhatsApp is named in at least one Voice.ai listing. No source specifically tested Telegram Desktop, WhatsApp Desktop or Google Meet. Mac support:
- **Windows + Mac:** Voicemod, Shift, HitPaw, MagicMic (separate licences on some); Dubbing AI has a Mac/iOS App Store app.
- **Windows-only for real time:** Altered desktop, VoxBooster, FineVoice desktop, and per some sources Voice.ai.

### Cited Findings
- **Voicemod:** "Free Real Time Voice Changer for PC & Mac" — [voicemod.net](https://www.voicemod.net/pricing/)
- **Shift:** Windows 10+ (64-bit) and macOS 10.14+ — [antinodeaudio.com/shift](https://antinodeaudio.com/shift)
- **HitPaw VoicePea:** Windows and macOS, with OS-specific licences — [HitPaw BR purchase page](https://www.hitpaw.com.br/purchase/buy-hitpaw-voice-changer.html)
- **Altered:** desktop real-time is Windows + NVIDIA only — [altered.ai/pricing](https://www.altered.ai/pricing/)
- **Dubbing AI:** works as an input device in Discord, Valorant or OBS — [dubbingai.io gaming page](https://dubbingai.io/articles/en/use-case/free-real-time-ai-voice-changer-gaming). It also has an App Store app — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6575362645)
- **Voice.ai:**
  - The desktop real-time changer works across "Discord, Zoom, … Skype, WhatsApp and TeamSpeak" (roundup claim) — [search summary of roundup](https://theairankings.com/best-ai-voice-changer/)
  - One review says real-time runs on Windows only, while the official site lists a Mac app (conflict) — [voice.ai llms.txt](https://voice.ai/llms.txt); [CloudTalk](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/voice-ai-review/)

### Inferences
- **Google Meet** runs in the browser, so the virtual mic is chosen in Meet's or Chrome's audio settings. It should work with any virtual-mic changer.
- **Telegram Desktop and WhatsApp Desktop** both expose a microphone selector in call settings, so they should accept a virtual mic. This is not verified by a source.
- Built-in noise suppression or "voice isolation" can degrade or gate changed voices: Discord's Krisp, Meet's noise cancellation, and similar features. Users typically disable it. This is a general practice claim, not sourced here.

### Gaps
- No source tested these apps specifically in Telegram Desktop, WhatsApp Desktop or Google Meet, or documented any incompatibility (e.g., exclusive-mode audio conflicts).
- macOS virtual-driver requirements (e.g., system extension approval on Apple Silicon) weren't covered for each app.

## 6. Newer 2025–2026 entrants and corporate changes

### Takeaway
I found no major new paid consumer entrant in 2025–26 with independent coverage. The notable changes:
- **Supertone Shift → "Shift" by Antinode Audio**, a new company. Supertone Play, API and Voice Builder ended Aug 31, 2026.
- **Voice.ai** has moved its pricing toward credit plans for voice agents and TTS.
- **Free local engines** (Beatrice, Echo Live, W-Okada) are competing on latency.

### Cited Findings
- Shift is "Formerly Supertone Shift," now sold by Antinode Audio Co., Ltd., and the supertone.ai/shift URL 301-redirects to antinodeaudio.com — [antinodeaudio.com/shift](https://antinodeaudio.com/shift)
- Supertone support said Shift, Clear and Air continue under a new company, while Play, API and Voice Builder were discontinued Aug 31, 2026 — [Supertone support (search snippet)](https://support.supertone.ai/hc/articles/16940735914639)
- Voice.ai's pricing tiers now centre on credits, concurrency, voice agents and phone numbers — [voice.ai/pricing](https://voice.ai/pricing)
- Beatrice (free, local, about 10 ms claimed, Windows, recently updated) — [GitHub](https://github.com/Project-Beatrice-V2/Beatrice-voicechanger-windows)
- Echo Live (free, Windows/macOS, GPU-accelerated via DirectML, CUDA and CoreML) — [voicechanger.live](https://voicechanger.live/)
