# Paid voice changers sell convenience, not realism

As of November 2026, the best paid voice changer that works live in a call is **Shift by Antinode Audio (formerly Supertone Shift)**. It offers neural character voices and a vendor-claimed **47 ms latency**, needs **no GPU**, runs on Windows and Mac, and starts at **$3.99 a month**. The best value is **VoxBooster**: **$23.99 a year or $40.99 for a lifetime licence**, about **10 cents per hour** at 20 hours of calls a month, with a 3-day trial that needs no card. **HitPaw VoicePea** is the runner-up for Mac users or anyone who wants a 30-day refund window. For an **HP Victus 15 (i7-13620H, RTX 5060 8 GB, 16 GB RAM) running Deep-Live-Cam**, Shift is again the pick. It runs on the CPU, so the face swap, which alone needs 6 GB or more of video memory (VRAM), keeps the whole graphics card. All of these are judgement calls on thin evidence. **No independent blind listening test or measured latency comparison of paid real-time changers exists**, and almost every latency figure comes from a vendor. The free benchmark has not moved: **w-okada running an RVC voice model** on an NVIDIA GPU is still the community's pick for realism. So on a PC whose GPU has nothing else to do, paying buys ease of setup, not better sound. The famous premium names belong in a separate category. **ElevenLabs and Fish Audio convert recordings, not live calls**, and ElevenLabs works out to about **$10 per hour of audio**, against 10–75 cents for the flat-rate live apps. Trust is the category's weak spot. Voice.ai scores 1.3/5 on Trustpilot, Voicemod scores 1.6/5, and the company that built Shift was liquidated in July 2026. So trial first, pay monthly, buy only from official sites, and use these tools only openly and with consent.

## No blind test exists, so every ranking is a judgement call

The evidence problem shapes everything below, so it comes first. This research found **no blind A/B or MOS listening study** (a mean opinion score test) and **no lab measurement of mic-to-output latency** comparing paid real-time voice changers. It also found no 2025–2026 review of them from TechRadar, PCMag or Tom's Guide. Most "best voice changer 2026" pages are written by companies that rank their own product first: Dubbing AI ([Dubbing AI](https://dubbingai.io/articles/en/list/best-real-time-voice-changers-compared)), iMyFone ([iMyFone](https://filme.imyfone.com/video-editing-software/best-real-time-voice-changer-software/)), EaseUS ([EaseUS](https://voicechanger.easeus.com/)) and VoxBooster, which runs dozens of "X vs VoxBooster" posts ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voicemod/)). The nearest thing to a multi-tool test is fast.io's "10 tools tested," updated 20 May 2026. It ran one 60-second sample through each tool, but used **no listener panel and no blind protocol**, and its "above 4.5" MOS scores for ElevenLabs and Respeecher are borrowed from Toolworthy ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/)). The most widely cited roundup of Reddit opinion concludes that "there is no single Reddit-approved winner," and warns that "user reports are not independent product tests" ([Real AI Test](https://www.realaitest.com/article/best-ai-voice-changer-reddit.html)). Public benchmarks exist for text-to-speech latency and quality, but not for voice conversion ([async.com](https://async.com/blog/tts-latency-vs-quality-benchmark/)).

What remains is a loose consensus on realism, and it puts a free tool on top. Free local RVC, run through w-okada or one of its forks with a well-trained voice model on an NVIDIA GPU, is the community's realism pick. Voice.ai's community models come next: Reddit summaries credit them with "startling realism," though they are "significantly more GPU-intensive" ([parse.gl](https://parse.gl/topics/real-time-ai-voice-changer)), and fast.io found them "noticeably more natural" than preset changers ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/)). Voicemod sits at the bottom. Its effects "lean toward novelty and gaming rather than realistic transformation" ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/)), and Trustpilot reviewers call some of its voices robotic and laggy ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/voicemod.net)). Between those poles, the evidence is thin. fast.io says Altered "can sound slightly robotic on longer passages" and that MagicMic's quality "varies across different voice presets" ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/)). VoxBooster itself admits that cloud "celebrity-sounding" voices have a polish its smaller local models cannot match ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voxbooster-vs-voice-ai-detailed-2026/)). Shift's only evidence is mixed Product Hunt reviews that praise its ease of use, low latency and realistic voices ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/supertone-shift/reviews)). Dubbing AI has no independent realism assessment at all. Every live tool trades some fidelity for speed. MetaVoice says so plainly when it sends users who want "superior quality" from its live app to its offline Studio ([MetaVoice](https://metavoice.notion.site/MetaVoice-Live-Setup-Guide-FAQs-ba0f672c2e244632adbb828bb4d27b99)).

Latency evidence is even weaker. Dubbing AI has published both "sub-30ms" ([Dubbing AI](https://dubbingai.io/articles/en/list/best-real-time-voice-changers-compared)) and "just 300 ms" ([Dubbing AI](https://dubbingai.io/articles/en/list/top-real-time-voice-changers)). Voice.ai's latency is put at **anywhere from under 50 ms to 500 ms**, depending on whether fast.io, Dubbing AI or VoxBooster is reporting, and the last two sell competing products ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voxbooster-vs-voice-ai-detailed-2026/)). The only hard community numbers are for free RVC: w-okada reaches **42 ms in a hand-tuned setup but 170–213 ms in the prebuilt app on an RTX 3070** ([AI Hub](https://docs.aihub.gg/realtime-voice-changer/local/deiteris-w-okada-fork/)). Against those numbers, claims of under 30 ms for neural conversion most likely measure audio buffer size or simple pitch effects, not full AI conversion. Voicemod's reported 12–20 ms probably falls into that category. A vendor blog's rule of thumb is a more realistic expectation: **25–45 ms for simple pitch effects, 80–130 ms for AI on a GPU, and 280–380 ms for AI on a CPU** ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/real-time-ai-voice-changer)). fast.io treats anything above 100 ms as "noticeable lag" ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/)), and Discord, Meet or WhatsApp add their own network delay on top. The one official, specific latency figure from a live product is Altered's **135 ms "algorithmic latency"** ([Altered](https://www.altered.ai/faqs/what-is-the-latency-of-the-real-time-voice-changer)).

Discord is every vendor's documented target, and Altered names Discord, Google Meet and WhatsApp explicitly ([Altered](https://altered.ai/real-time-pro)). No source tested Telegram Desktop specifically. Most of these tools are **flat subscriptions or lifetime licences with unlimited use**. Only three meter usage, each in a different way. Altered caps its cloud "online voice morphing" at 3, 60 or 180 minutes a month ([Altered](https://www.altered.ai/pricing/)). Shift uses credits to unlock voices and sells permanent licences per voice ([Antinode Audio](https://antinodeaudio.com/shift)). Voice.ai sells credit plans for text-to-speech and voice agents, but does not say what its desktop real-time changer costs ([Voice.ai](https://voice.ai/pricing)).

### Shift wins on balance, not on proof

Shift was built by Supertone, the voice-AI company owned by HYBE. It is now sold by **Antinode Audio Co., Ltd.** of Daejeon, Korea, labelled "Formerly Supertone Shift." It is at version 2.1, runs on 64-bit Windows 10+ and macOS 10.14+, and promises conversion "on any device, no GPU needed" ([Antinode Audio](https://antinodeaudio.com/shift)). Its Japanese page claims **47 ms latency**, "among the shortest in the industry," but nobody has checked that independently ([Supertone JP](https://supertone.ai/ja/shift)). The English page lists four tiers:

| Tier | Price |
|---|---|
| Free | $0, with 3,000 credits to unlock voices |
| Starter | $3.99/month; promo: "all 40 voices" for $1.99 |
| Pro | $14.99/month |
| Permanent | **$149.99 per voice** |

Source: ([Antinode Audio](https://antinodeaudio.com/shift)). Two cautions follow. First, the page never says whether credits are spent per voice or per minute, so the real hourly cost could be higher than the table shows. Second, the Japanese page describes a different structure, with $9 a month per voice, $179 per voice permanently, and a $49 licence ([Supertone JP](https://supertone.ai/ja/shift)). Take Shift monthly, not as a permanent per-voice purchase.

### VoxBooster and HitPaw sell unlimited use for pennies an hour

These two offer the cheapest unlimited licences with official, published prices. VoxBooster costs **$6.99 a month, $23.99 a year or $40.99 for life**. It has a 3-day full trial with no card and takes card, PayPal or crypto ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/pricing)). It runs the voice model on your own Windows PC and claims "typical end-to-end latency under 300ms on a mid-range PC" ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voxbooster-vs-voice-ai-detailed-2026/)).

HitPaw's Windows Standard plan costs $29.95 a year or $49.95 permanently. Its Pro plan costs **$39.95 a year or $65.95 permanently** and explicitly lists the "Real-time AI voice changer… Unlimited Usage." Both come with a 30-day money-back guarantee ([HitPaw](https://www.hitpaw.com/purchase/buy-hitpaw-voice-changer.html)). Mac licences are sold separately ([HitPaw BR](https://www.hitpaw.com.br/purchase/buy-hitpaw-voice-changer.html)), and the Mac app needs an internet connection for real-time conversion ([FileHorse](https://mac.filehorse.com/download-hitpaw-voice-changer/)). Toolworthy, an affiliate review site, puts HitPaw's latency at 80–120 ms ([Toolworthy](https://www.toolworthy.ai/blog/best-ai-voice-changer)). Neither tool has an independent realism review, so "best value" assumes it passes your own trial.

### Voicemod, Altered and the bottom three each fall short differently

**Voicemod** is the name everyone knows, but it is not the pick for realism. The free tier offers "a limited selection [of voices] that rotates daily." PRO unlocks the full library, VoiceLab and unlimited soundboards ([Voicemod](https://support.voicemod.net/hc/en-us/articles/360014300760-Voicemod-Features-Free-vs-PRO)), though VoiceLab chains effects rather than cloning voices. Voicemod publishes no fixed price. Plans "are shown in the app and vary by account and region," and checkout runs through the payment processors Xsolla or Paddle ([Voicemod](https://support.voicemod.net/hc/en-us/articles/360013354920-How-to-purchase-a-Voicemod-license)). Third-party price figures range from **about $2.49 to $12 a month, and $39.99 to $99.99 for lifetime** ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/); [Lemonsight](https://lemonsight.com/tool/audio/voicemod)). Its AI voices need a CPU with AVX2 support ([Real AI Test](https://www.realaitest.com/article/best-ai-voice-changer-reddit.html)).

**Altered** is the only vendor with official specifications, but it costs five to ten times more. Creator is $30 a month on annual billing, with 60 cloud minutes; Professional is $90 a month, with 180. Desktop minutes are shown as unlimited, although "unlimited local morphing" appears as a Professional feature. There is a one-week trial with no card ([Altered](https://www.altered.ai/pricing/)). Its Studio desktop real-time mode needs a Windows PC with an **8 GB+ NVIDIA GPU**. A separate RealTime Pro page promises "commodity CPUs" plus optional cloud computing, but its footer still reads 2022–2023 ([Altered](https://altered.ai/real-time-pro)).

**MagicMic** sells for $9.99 a month, $45.99 a year or $59.99 for life, discounted from list prices of $15.99, $79.99 and $99.99 ([iMyFone](https://filme.imyfone.com/voice-changer/pricing/)). It does not document its latency or where it processes audio.

**Dubbing AI** shows no readable price list on its pricing page ([Dubbing AI](https://dubbingai.io/pricing)). Third parties quote $9.99 a month or $59.99 a year ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/best-ai-voice-changers-2026/)), which conflicts with App Store listings of $8 a month or $48 a year for Standard, and $14 or $84 for Premium ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6575362645)). It claims "on-device AI processing" at 2–3% CPU and says it "requires an internet connection for initial voice loading" ([Dubbing AI](https://dubbingai.io/articles/en/list/best-real-time-voice-changers-compared)).

**Voice.ai** probably has the most realistic paid voices but still ranks last. Its pricing page now sells credit plans for text-to-speech and voice agents, at $5–$880 a month, and does not price the desktop changer ([Voice.ai](https://voice.ai/pricing)). Users report a "FREE 3-day trial, followed by a $7.99 charge," and a voice bought "for 4.6k coins" that they "can't even use without a subscription" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/voice.ai)). Its live mode "demands a dedicated NVIDIA GPU" ([CloudTalk](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/voice-ai-review/)). Its community models are very likely RVC-class, so anyone who owns the GPU Voice.ai needs can run the same kind of model free in w-okada, without the billing risk.

## ElevenLabs and Fish Audio convert recordings, not calls

The companies with the best reputations for voice quality have **not shipped a live voice changer you can use in a call**. They belong in a separate category from everything above.

ElevenLabs' own documentation describes transforming "recorded or uploaded" audio in segments of up to **5 minutes** ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/product/speech-synthesis/speech-to-speech)). Its "stream" API takes the whole input file and streams back only the output ([ElevenLabs API](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/api-reference/speech-to-speech/stream.md)). A "Conversational Voice Changer" page promises "real-time" in its search description, but the page itself describes uploading audio and then downloading a file ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/voice-changer/conversational)). ElevenLabs' own Zoom guide tells live callers to route audio through a third-party tool such as Voicemod ([ElevenLabs blog](https://elevenlabs.io/blog/the-best-voice-changer-for-zoom)).

Fish Audio's voice changer is a browser upload where "most clips process in under 30 seconds" ([Fish Audio](https://fish.audio/voice-changer/)). Its much-promoted "Live 3" is a full-duplex model for conversational agents, not a voice changer ([Fish Audio](https://fish.audio)).

The same pattern holds elsewhere. Respeecher says "real-time conversion is not available in Voice Marketplace." It offers real-time only "with a customer plan," at a price "considerably more" than the standard plans ([Respeecher](https://www.respeecher.com/faq)). Resemble's speech-to-speech API takes a WAV file and returns a clip, with a limit of 50 MB or 5 minutes ([Resemble](https://docs.resemble.ai/voice-generation/speech-to-speech)). Its live socket server is sample code for developers ([ecosyste.ms](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fresemble-ai%2Fresemble-live-sts-socket)).

The cost gap is as wide as the latency gap. ElevenLabs charges its voice changer at **1,000 credits per minute** ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)). The $6 Starter plan therefore covers only 30 minutes a month, and the $99 Pro plan covers 10 hours.

At about $10 an hour, ElevenLabs costs **roughly 13 to 120 times more per hour** than the flat-rate live apps, which run 10–75 cents. That price alone would rule it out for calls, before latency is even considered.

Krisp and Sanas are genuinely live, but they solve a different problem. Their accent converters keep **your own identity** and change only your accent, and Krisp's output is US English only ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/ai/new-ai-tech-can-change-your-accent-but-only-to-north-american)). They are not persona changers.

A do-it-yourself pipeline is also possible: cut microphone audio into chunks and send them to ElevenLabs' streaming endpoint. It would add a network round trip on top of the model's processing time and cost $7–12 an hour, and no one has published a latency measurement for it.

## An 8 GB RTX 5060 cannot comfortably host two neural models

The HP Victus 15 meets the minimum for live face swapping, but only just. Deep-Live-Cam's official prebuilt edition needs **"6GB VRAM or higher"** ([Gumroad](https://hacksider.gumroad.com/l/vccdmm)). Even an RTX 4080 Laptop GPU with 12 GB managed only **about 15 FPS** with stock Deep-Live-Cam at 960×540 ([VerSprite](https://versprite.com/security-resources/real-time-deepfake-face-swapping)). A September 2026 setup guide recommends **16 GB or more of system RAM** for live mode ([PromptZone](https://www.promptzone.com/damonwho/deep-live-cam-setup-tutorial-c0c.md)), which is exactly what the Victus has. Any voice changer that also runs on the GPU competes with the face swap for both memory and processing time.

| Workload on the Victus 15 | GPU demand | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Deep-Live-Cam live swap | 6 GB+ VRAM minimum | [Gumroad](https://hacksider.gumroad.com/l/vccdmm) |
| w-okada / deiteris RVC | ~1 GB VRAM (FP16), ~2 GB (FP32) | [deiteris](https://github.com/deiteris/voice-changer) |
| Altered Studio desktop real-time | Needs an 8 GB+ NVIDIA GPU by itself | [Altered](https://www.altered.ai/pricing/) |
| Voice.ai live mode | Dedicated NVIDIA GPU | [CloudTalk](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/voice-ai-review/) |
| Shift / Voicemod AI voices | None: "no GPU needed" / AVX2 CPU | [Antinode Audio](https://antinodeaudio.com/shift); [Real AI Test](https://www.realaitest.com/article/best-ai-voice-changer-reddit.html) |
| **Available on the RTX 5060** | **8 GB, also used by Windows and OBS** | — |

On paper, Deep-Live-Cam plus an FP16 RVC model would take 7 GB or more of the 8 GB card before Windows and OBS take their share. **No published benchmark tests the two together on one GPU.** The free voice tool VonoVox shows the risk: its "audio can stutter if the GPU is busy" ([Real AI Test](https://www.realaitest.com/article/best-ai-voice-changer-reddit.html)). The GPU-based paid options are worse. Altered Studio needs a whole 8 GB card by itself. Voice.ai wants the GPU too, and its free credits are tied to background "distributed computing" that borrows your hardware ([CloudTalk](https://www.cloudtalk.io/blog/voice-ai-review/)), the last thing a GPU-bound laptop needs. VoxBooster's own blog calls an RTX 3060 "the practical minimum" for comfortable AI voice changing ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/real-time-ai-voice-changer)). That suggests the value pick also leans on the GPU, which is why it is not the recommendation for this laptop.

### Run the voice on the CPU and test under load before paying

**Run Shift on the CPU and give Deep-Live-Cam the whole graphics card.** The 10-core i7-13620H has headroom, but reviewers describe Shift as "quite CPU-heavy" ([Softpedia](https://www.softpedia.com/get/Multimedia/Audio/Audio-Editors-Recorders/Supertone-Shift.shtml)), and a laptop CPU slows itself down when the GPU next to it runs hot. Test in real conditions. Keep the laptop plugged in, turn Deep-Live-Cam's face enhancer off, lower its capture resolution, and watch CPU load in Task Manager during a 20-minute test call. If Shift stutters, the CPU-based fallback is Voicemod's AI voices. They sound less realistic, and some users report high RAM use ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/voicemod.net)). Do not choose Dubbing AI for its "0% GPU" claim. Near-zero CPU use is implausible for neural conversion running on your own machine. If the audio is actually processed on Dubbing AI's servers, its privacy policy says nothing about how live voice audio is handled ([Dubbing AI](https://dubbingai.io/privacy-policy)).

### Free w-okada might fit in 1 GB, but nobody has tested it alongside a face swap

The free alternative deserves a fair comparison. w-okada is MIT-licensed and costs nothing ([Real AI Test](https://www.realaitest.com/article/best-ai-voice-changer-reddit.html)). On a PC whose GPU has nothing else to do, it matches or beats every paid app on realism, once you have tuned it and found a good voice model. Finding models got harder after Weights.gg, the largest RVC model library, shut down around 1 April 2026 ([aidirectory](https://aidirectory.com/news/weights-gg-shuts-down-coreweave-weights-and-biases-acquisition-clarification)). On the Victus, an FP16 model of about 1 GB might fit beside Deep-Live-Cam, but that is untested. The official w-okada builds were last updated in August 2025 ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/wok000/vcclient000)), so confirm they run on a brand-new RTX 50-series card; Deep-Live-Cam's README already targets CUDA 12.8 ([GitHub](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)). The sensible order is to try Shift's free tier alongside the face swap, then free w-okada in FP16 the same way, and pay only for whichever holds up.

### Each call app needs its own noise filter switched off

Call apps add their own processing, and it can damage a converted voice. In **Discord**, set Noise Suppression to "None" so Krisp does not treat the changed voice as noise ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voice-changer-discord-krisp-conflict)). **Google Meet's** noise cancellation is **on by default for paid Workspace and Google One 2TB+ accounts**; turn it off under Settings → Audio ([Google](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9919960)).

**WhatsApp Desktop** is murkier. A vendor reports that it accepts a virtual microphone ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voice-changer-whatsapp-video-desktop)), but an older quote from WhatsApp's own FAQ says "virtual audio and video devices aren't supported" ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/obs-virtual-cam-is-not-working-with-whatsapp-desktop-app.144629/post-529441)). In practice, the **camera** side is what breaks: OBS Virtual Camera usually does not appear. The reported Windows fix is the DroidCam Virtual Output plugin for OBS ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/new-whatsapp-desktop-application-does-not-work-with-obs-virtual-camera.140414/post-671686)).

None of this works inside native phone apps. Place the call from the desktop app, and the other person can still answer on a phone. Voice and video will arrive with different delays, so clap on camera to see which one is ahead, then delay the earlier stream with OBS's offset settings.

## Billing traps and consent rules decide the real risk

### Voice.ai and Voicemod draw the most billing complaints

The best-documented risks are about money and conduct, not sound. **Voice.ai** scores **1.3/5 on Trustpilot** in a November 2026 snapshot of 227 reviews, 88% of them one-star. Reviewers describe charges after a "free" trial, renewals "without any reminder or warning," "no option to cancel your payment on the website," and the payment processor Xsolla refusing refunds ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/voice.ai)). In 2023 a developer found open-source code licensed under the GPL in Voice.ai's app without the required source code. He was banned from its Discord after raising it ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2023/02/08/voiceai_open_source)).

**Voicemod** scores **1.6/5 from 459 reviews**, even though more than half of its reviews ever posted are five-star. Trustpilot weights recent reviews, and the recent ones centre on unanswered refunds and paywalls. Voicemod confirmed on Trustpilot that its annual plan is **charged upfront even when the app shows a monthly figure** ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/voicemod.net)). One independent review says Voicemod's policy lets it use voice samples for "training our technologies," with no clear opt-out, while Voicemod says it does not listen to real-time conversations ([BlockSentient](https://blocksentient.com/review/voicemod)). No Voicemod data breach was found. Its official installers are signed with a DigiCert certificate ([Gridinsoft](https://gridinsoft.com/online-virus-scanner/id/3d50ce7693b23cd3d1f05682d0fec9958ab1329a2d01e6d6c79bdb2e6505984c)), and the adware flagged in one sandbox test came from a third-party bundled installer ([ANY.RUN](https://any.run/report/afd7452c34570e409fc0c2bc8a22fb7429a3cc8f48e85fe6a154656ec020330d/126229bc-1e98-489f-a0e4-124b790d96c6)).

**Dubbing AI** has only 23 Trustpilot reviews, averaging 2.3/5. They include charges after cancelling and a Pro upgrade that never activated ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/dubbingai.io)). **Shift** has a continuity risk rather than complaints. Supertone's shareholders voted to dissolve the company in July 2026, after HYBE had invested about $35M ([Music Business Worldwide](https://musicbusinessworldwide.com/hybe-winds-down-ai-voice-company-supertone-after-investing-nearly-35m)). Shift's new product page carries no privacy statement.

### Trial first, pay monthly, and buy lifetime only after a month

Start with the free trials: VoxBooster's 3-day trial with no card, Altered's one-week trial with no card, Shift's free tier with 3,000 credits, or Krisp's 7-day trial with no card ([Krisp](https://krisp.ai/pricing/)). Test inside a real Discord or Meet call, not just the app's preview. Pay monthly for the first month. Move to a lifetime licence (VoxBooster at $40.99, HitPaw at $49.95–65.95) only once the tool has proved itself. A lifetime licence removes renewal risk, but the product can still change: a 2019 Voicemod lifetime buyer now complains it is "full of AI everywhere" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/voicemod.net)).

Three traps stand out. First, annual plans displayed as monthly prices. Second, HitPaw's "1 year paid monthly" plan, which cannot be cancelled during its 12 months ([HitPaw](https://www.hitpaw.com/purchase/buy-hitpaw-voice-changer.html)). Third, Shift's $149.99 per-voice permanent licences while its ownership is still settling. With Xsolla and Paddle, which act as the seller of record, the real renewal terms appear only on the checkout screen, and refunds go through them rather than the app maker ([Voicemod](https://support.voicemod.net/hc/en-us/articles/360013354920-How-to-purchase-a-Voicemod-license)). Read that screen before paying. Voicemod subscriptions are cancelled at account.voicemod.net ([Datalevo](https://datalevo.com/?p=5194)).

Buy only from official sites. That rules out "Voicemod PRO lifetime" accounts resold on Gameflip and GGSEL ([Gameflip](https://gameflip.com/item/voicemod-pro-lifetime-to-your-account/e405c7ba-84cf-49d4-a5d6-4b637fd09a4d); [GGSEL](https://ggsel.net/en/catalog/voicemod-pro)), and GitHub repositories named "AI-Voice-Changer" that link to unexplained .zip downloads ([GitHub](https://github.com/delugebaronpace/AI-Voice-Changer)).

### Personas are legal; deceiving people as someone real is not

The legal line is the same whether a tool is paid or free. **Playing a persona or staying anonymous is broadly allowed; deceiving people as a real person is not.** Discord bans misrepresenting your identity "in a deceptive or harmful way" and requires parody to be clearly labelled ([Discord](https://discord.com/guidelines)). In the US, the FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices count as "artificial" under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, so calls that use them need prior express consent ([FCC](https://WWW.FCC.GOV/document/fcc-makes-ai-generated-voices-robocalls-illegal)). Tennessee's ELVIS Act adds simulated voices to the state's right-of-publicity law ([Holland & Knight](https://hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2024/04/first-of-its-kind-ai-law-addresses-deep-fakes-and-voice-clones)). From **2 August 2026**, the EU AI Act requires deployers who generate or manipulate deepfake audio to disclose that it is AI-made ([Technology.org](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/)).

Some vendors draw their own lines. Respeecher demands written consent from the voice owner ([Respeecher](https://www.respeecher.com/faq)), ElevenLabs blocks cloning of high-risk "no-go" voices ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/legal/what-are-no-go-voices)), and Deep-Live-Cam's README asks for consent and clear labelling whenever a real person's face is used ([GitHub](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)). In practice, tell the people on the call, use fictional or licensed voices, and never use these tools for money requests, identity checks or romance.

## Conclusion

The live voice-changer market is priced like a cheap utility, at 10–75 cents an hour. Premium voice conversion costs $7–60 an hour and still cannot run inside a call. That gap shows that the hard engineering problem, high-quality conversion streamed with low delay, has not yet been turned into a product by the companies with the best models. Fish Audio's full-duplex "Live 3" agent model and its $52M funding round ([noqta](https://noqta.tn/en/news/fish-audio-52m-seed-voice-ai-s21-pro-2026)) are worth watching as a sign that could change. Until it does, the case for paying is narrow. **Paid apps earn their fee when the GPU is unavailable**: on a Mac, on a weak PC, or, as on the Victus, when a face swap has claimed the graphics card. Wherever a free NVIDIA GPU exists, GPU-dependent paid apps like Voice.ai lose to free RVC on both cost and trust.

The second lesson concerns vendor fragility. Supertone has been liquidated, Weights.gg has shut down, Voice.ai has turned its pricing toward business voice agents, and Shift's two price lists disagree. In this market the bigger buying risk is not a bad voice but a product that changes or disappears after you have paid. That argues for short commitments, with a one-off lifetime licence only when it costs less than about two years of subscription. And with the EU's deepfake disclosure duty now in force, using a clearly labelled persona openly is becoming a legal requirement as well as good manners.
