# Tavus, Gemini and Zoom set 2026's calling bar

As of November 2026, the most realistic AI you can video call is **Tavus's Conversational Video Interface** (Phoenix-4/4.5 face, Raven-1 perception, Sparrow-2 turn-taking). It is rated 1080p/40 fps and costs about $0.24–0.37 per minute. **Anam's Cara-4.1 Express** is the best balance of realism, speed and price, at about $0.11–0.16 per minute and the fastest measured start-up. For AI voice, there is no single winner. On human-preference rankings, **Cartesia's managed agents, Google's Gemini 3.8 Live and xAI's Grok Voice 3.0** lead, and Grok is the value pick at $0.08 per minute. **ChatGPT Voice (GPT-Live-1.1)** is the strongest mainstream consumer app, and **Sesame** is the one reviewers keep calling the most human-sounding. For calls between people, live camera video in mainstream apps still tops out at **1080p**. **Zoom with its $25/month Enhanced Media add-on** (1080p at 60 fps, 8–12 Mbps) is the highest published spec. **Google Meet** has the best built-in AI, with live speech translation in 63 languages. Three caveats run through all of this. Most resolution and latency figures are vendor claims. Network transport, not the model, often caps the quality you actually see. And even the best production AI avatars are still spotted as AI by almost everyone within about 20 seconds. In other words, the "highest video quality" race in AI calling is now about believability and responsiveness more than pixel count.

## Tavus looks most human; Anam answers fastest

Real-time avatar platforms have split into three tiers. At the premium end, **Tavus** launched Phoenix-4 on 18 February 2026, calling it "the first real-time human rendering model with emotional intelligence" ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260218278213/en/Tavus-Launches-Phoenix-4-the-First-Real-Time-Human-Rendering-Model-with-Emotional-Intelligence)). Tavus specifies **1080p at 40 fps**. The model uses a 3D Gaussian Splatting renderer, shows active-listening behaviour, and expresses nine named emotion states ([Tavus](https://www.tavus.io/blog/phoenix-4-real-time-human-rendering-with-emotional-intelligence)). It is paired with Raven-1, which reads the user's tone and facial expression through the camera, and the whole system runs **under 600 ms end to end** over WebRTC ([MarkTechPost](https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/02/18/tavus-launches-phoenix-4-a-gaussian-diffusion-model-bringing-real-time-emotional-intelligence-and-sub-600ms-latency-to-generative-video-ai/)). In August, Tavus added Sparrow-2, an audio-native turn-taking model it says fails in only **2.1% of conversations** ([Tavus](https://tavus.io/blog/sparrow-2)). A Phoenix-4.5 update reportedly improves lip sync by 16%, though no official launch post exists ([TAAFT](https://theresanaiforthat.com/model/phoenix-4-5/)).

**Anam** competes on speed. Its Cara-4 model (July 2026) animates a single photo at 25 fps, with about **150 ms of server-side generation** ([Anam](https://anam.ai/resources/glossary/real-time-avatar)). Cara-4.1 Express followed on 20 October as its "most expressive realtime avatar model," adding finer performance control ([Anam changelog](https://anam.ai/docs/changelog)). It outputs **1152×768** ([talking-avatar.dev](https://talking-avatar.dev/compare/avatar-api-pricing)).

**HeyGen LiveAvatar** streams at **720p by default**. 1080p is available only on request, needs 1080p source footage, and adds latency ([HeyGen FAQ](https://help.heygen.com/en/articles/12758866-liveavatar-faq)).

The only credible independent hands-on test is WebRTC.ventures' October 2026 comparison of four vendors. It found a gap between specs and what reaches the screen. **Tavus and HeyGen both arrived at 1280×720 / 25 fps**, Anam at 1152×768, and Simli at 512×512. All four were delivered at about 400 kbps VP8 ([WebRTC.ventures](https://webrtc.ventures/2026/10/real-time-ai-avatar-pipecat-interruption-comparison/)). The tester ranked realism **"Tavus, then Anam."** Anam was fastest to show its first frame (3.5–4.1 s versus 8.1–9.8 s for Tavus), and both Anam and Simli stopped talking after an interruption in about 1.0 s, against 1.3–1.4 s for Tavus ([WebRTC.ventures](https://webrtc.ventures/2026/10/real-time-ai-avatar-pipecat-interruption-comparison/)). A January 2026 blind study of 178 people put **Anam first on every dimension** and found that responsiveness predicted user experience better than visual quality. Anam commissioned that study, however ([Anam](https://anam.ai/blog/best-ai-avatar-platforms-refresh-draft)). HeyGen's own ranking scores itself first (9.1), ahead of Tavus (8.1) and Anam (8.0), but it ran no live sessions ([HeyGen](https://www.heygen.com/blog/best-real-time-ai-avatar-platforms)).

Tavus's own October Turing test shows how far the technology still has to go. Only **1 of 41 participants (2.4%)** thought the production Phoenix-4.5 stack was human, and most doubters spotted the AI within about 20 seconds. The unreleased **Griffin-Lite** model, by contrast, fooled **48%** ([explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/tavus-griffin-video-turing-test-him-2026)). Griffin is a full-duplex, video-to-video model that outputs 720p at 25 fps with about 0.43 s audio-to-video latency, but it is "not available for use for customers at this time" ([explainx.ai](https://explainx.ai/blog/tavus-griffin-video-turing-test-him-2026)).

### Niche players fill specific gaps

Several products serve needs the leaders do not. **D-ID V4** (March 2026) claims output up to **4K** and includes a camera layer that feeds the user's nonverbal cues to the LLM ([eLearning Industry](https://elearningindustry.com/press-releases/d-id-launches-v4-expressive-visual-agents-for-real-time-ai-interaction)). No one has independently measured it, and at $0.50–0.56 per minute it is among the most expensive options ([talking-avatar.dev](https://talking-avatar.dev/compare/avatar-api-pricing)). **LemonSlice** is the only provider that animates the **entire body** ([LemonSlice](https://lemonslice.com/i/best-real-time-avatar)). **Beyond Presence** offers 1080p on every plan and has beta support for joining Zoom, Meet, Teams and Webex calls ([talking-avatar.dev](https://talking-avatar.dev/compare/avatar-api-pricing)). **Google's Gemini 3.8 Live with Live Avatar** (24 September) supports **97 languages**, with lip sync that follows mid-conversation language switches. It can also see the user, but it is limited to Gemini Enterprise and Google has not published its resolution or latency ([Google](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-3-8-live-with-live-avatar?output=1)). **Synthesia Sessions** launched on 1 October for roleplay and survey conversations ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/synthesia-launches-sessions-platform-for-interactive-ai-avatars/)). Avoid **Hedra's realtime avatar**, which shut down on 15 April 2026 ([LiveKit](https://docs.livekit.io/agents/models/avatar/plugins/hedra.md)).

## Voice AI leaders change depending on what you measure

For voice, the underlying speech-to-speech models rank differently depending on whether a benchmark rewards reasoning or human preference. Artificial Analysis's Speech Agent Arena measures preference with an Elo score anchored so that GPT-Realtime-1.5 equals 1000. It puts **Cartesia Managed Agents first (1111 Elo, 91.7% task success)**, followed by **Gemini 3.1 Flash Live Minimal (1089)**, **Gemini 3.8 Live (1074 / 93.2%)** and **Grok Voice 3.0 (1059 / 94.8%)**. OpenAI's GPT-Live-1 comes next (1027–1037), and ElevenLabs' new Eleven Realtime v1 Max is mid-pack (990) ([AA Arena](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-speech/arena)).

OpenAI's reasoning-heavy **GPT-Realtime-2** shows the trade-off most clearly. It tops Artificial Analysis's reasoning and agentic Speech-to-Speech Index (77.2%) ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/announcing-the-artificial-analysis-speech-to-speech-index)) but scores only about 920 on preference ([AA Arena](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-speech/arena)). Its "High" setting takes **2.33 s to first audio**, against 0.82 s for GPT-Realtime-1.5 ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/announcing-the-artificial-analysis-speech-to-speech-index)). On a live call, extra thinking costs naturalness.

Grok Voice 3.0 stands out on value. It launched on 14 October at **$0.08 per minute** ([Technobezz](https://www.technobezz.com/news/xai-launches-grok-voice-per-minute)) and combines top-five preference with the highest task success among the leaders. OpenAI's full-duplex GPT-Live-1, which "listens while it speaks," reached the API on 10 September at a reported ~$0.05 per minute ([Quartz](https://qz.com/openai-gpt-live-voice-models-listen-speak-070926)). Gemini 3.8 Live is now the default Live API model ([Google AI](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models)), but its reliability is uneven. Developers report the 3.1 Flash Live preview regressing to **9–15 s to first audio** from September ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-1-flash-live-preview-9-15-s-time-to-first-audio-since-2026-09-05-09-00-utc-was-1-s-some-turns-never-answer-reproduced-from-two-projects-two-networks/180918)). If you are planning a new build on **Hume EVI**, don't: its API is reportedly being retired on 13 November 2026 ([toolchase](https://toolchase.com/blog/hume-ai-review/)).

For pure voice quality inside a pipeline that chains separate speech-to-text, language and text-to-speech models, **ElevenLabs' Eleven v4 and v4 Turbo** hold #1 and #2 on Artificial Analysis's text-to-speech arena (1339 and 1335 Elo). Cartesia Sonic 3.6 (1297) is also near the top. **Speechify Simba 3.5** (1286 Elo, $6.58 per million characters) gives near-top quality at about a twelfth of Eleven v4's $80 ([AA TTS](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard)). Eleven v4 Turbo generates audio in about 100 ms ([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/elevenlabs-launches-eleven-v4-with-low-latency-turbo-variant/)). OpenAI's native realtime voice ranks only **#58** on that arena ([AA TTS](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard)).

### Real phone calls take 1–1.8 seconds per turn, not 400 ms

Managed phone-agent platforms advertise response times of 400–600 ms, but those figures cover only part of the pipeline. In independent caller-side measurements (Openbenchmarks, about 432 turns per platform, compiled by Telnyx, which competes with these platforms), median time to first audio was 1,296 ms for Telnyx, **1,424 ms for ElevenLabs**, 1,520 ms for Bland, 1,558 ms for Vapi and 1,740 ms for Retell. Effective costs ran from **$0.050 per minute (Telnyx) to $0.141 (Bland)**, with ElevenLabs at $0.079 and Vapi at $0.084 ([Telnyx](https://telnyx.com/resources/voice-ai-agents-compared-latency)). A separate October benchmark reproduced the ElevenLabs–Vapi–Retell order ([benchlm](https://benchlm.ai/best/voice-ai-agents)). A 500-call production study, by contrast, measured Retell (680 ms) and Vapi (720 ms) much faster. Tuning and deployment clearly move these numbers more than the choice of vendor ([Telnyx](https://telnyx.com/resources/voice-ai-agents-compared-latency)).

The platforms differ mainly in what they are built for. **Vapi** suits developers: it charges a $0.05 per minute platform fee and lets you bring your own models ([morphllm](https://www.morphllm.com/best-ai-voice-agent-platforms)). **Retell** targets call centres, with 20 concurrent calls included ([morphllm](https://www.morphllm.com/best-ai-voice-agent-platforms)). **Bland** charges a flat $0.14 per minute all-in ([superdupr](https://superdupr.com/blog/voice-ai-pricing)).

### Consumer apps: Sesame sounds most human, ChatGPT is the safest all-rounder

If you simply want to talk to an AI on your phone, there is no rigorous head-to-head test, but reviewers keep converging on the same names. PCWorld called **Sesame** "the best I've tested" and the most human-like conversation it had experienced ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3151873/sesame-ai-voice-app-is-the-best-ive-tested-thats-what-worries-me.html)). Sesame's 6 October update opened its app to everyone on iOS and Android ([artrealmai](https://artrealmai.com/article/sesame-october-update-new-voice-model-ios-android)), and it holds a 4.9/5 App Store rating ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sesame-personal-agents/id6756329076)). The downside is that its human-style fillers slow down quick factual questions, and language support is weak ([WeavAI](https://weavai.app/blog/en/2026/06/14/sesame-ai-review-2026-are-maya-and-miles-human-like/)).

**ChatGPT Voice** now runs on full-duplex GPT-Live-1. Free users get it through the mini model, and GPT-Live-1.1 has been rolling out since 15 October ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/introducing-gpt-live/); [mixed-news](https://mixed-news.com/en/chatgpt-voice-gpt-live-1-1-new-voices-german-japanese-hindi/)). That makes it the most capable mainstream voice assistant that also sounds natural.

## Human-to-human calls still top out at 1080p, so AI features decide the winner

For video calls between people, "4K calling" is mostly marketing. Zoom's official bandwidth documentation gives standard participant video as **1080p at 30 fps (4–6 Mbps)**. The **Enhanced Media add-on** raises that to **1080p at 60 fps (8–12 Mbps)**, with 1080p gallery tiles for up to four people. Meetings above 100 participants fall back to standard quality ([Zoom](https://library.zoom.com/technical-library/de/administrationsbereich/network-management/quality-of-service-and-network-best-practices-explainer/bandwidth-control-settings)). The add-on costs $25 per month on top of Workplace Pro at about $14 ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/zoom/diffs/2026W13)), and at least one paying user reports not getting Full HD despite following the setup steps ([Zoom Community](https://community.zoom.com/meetings-2/1080p-in-zoom-meetings-with-the-enhanced-media-add-on-configured-as-documented-still-not-getting-full-hd-82165)).

The other major apps sit at or below that level. Google Meet gives **1080p** only on premium plans, including consumer Google One AI Pro ([Google](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/10459644?hl=en)). Teams' documented 1080p is for town halls, a Teams Premium feature since January 2026 ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-nz/microsoftTeams/enable-1080p-video-resolution-town-hall)). Phone apps such as FaceTime and WhatsApp look broadly alike at around 720p, with FaceTime holding a slight edge on flagship phones; this comes from a low-reliability guide ([bestforandroid](https://bestforandroid.com/facetime-alternatives/)). Real 4K exists only in three places. The first is hardware: Cisco desk and room systems ([Cisco](https://cisco.com/c/en_in/products/collaboration-endpoints/webex-desk-camera/index.html)) and the **$24,999 HP Dimension with Google Beam**, a life-size 3D display now shipping in six countries for one-to-one calls ([shashi.co](https://www.shashi.co/2026/10/google-ships-life-size-beam-rooms-to.html)). The second is screen streaming, such as Discord Nitro's 4K60 AV1 ([NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/nvidia-studio-discord-av1/)). The third is local recording in studio tools such as Riverside and StreamYard Advanced ([talks.co](https://talks.co/p/streamyard-vs-riverside)).

Because video specs have converged, AI features now separate these apps. **Google Meet** leads on translation. Since 22 October 2026, its Gemini-powered speech translation covers **63 languages** with automatic language detection, each participant can switch it on individually, and the translation is spoken "in a voice like yours." Free Gmail accounts do not get it ([Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2026/10/real-time-speech-translation-in-google-Meet-expands-to-63-languages-with-automatic-language-detection.html); [Google](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/10459644?hl=en)). Teams' AI Interpreter requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot licence ([MC1267977](https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1267977)). Zoom's agentic AI Companion is included in paid plans ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/zoom/diffs/2025Q4)).

On audio, the latest lab data is old and vendor-funded. TestDevLab's 2024 benchmark, commissioned by Zoom, ranked **Zoom first overall and Webex first on audio**, with Webex strongest under heavy packet loss and Teams last ([Zoom report](https://www.zoom.com/en/resources/video-audio-quality-report/); [Techzine](https://www.techzine.eu/blogs/devops/124390/inside-testdevlab-the-company-that-breaks-tech-for-a-living)). Webex's AI Codec claims good audio at up to 16x lower bandwidth ([heise](https://heise.de/-9848886)). On iPhone, Apple's Voice Isolation mic mode removes background noise in FaceTime and in third-party apps such as WhatsApp ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/use-voice-isolation/)). No independent 2025–2026 test has measured delivered resolution, bitrate or audio quality (MOS score) across these apps, so these rankings are directional only.

## Conclusion

The headline numbers are misleading in both AI video and human calling. A 4K or 1080p40 avatar still arrives as roughly 720p at about 400 kbps, and a "400 ms" voice agent still takes about 1.5 s per turn on a real phone line. Buyers should therefore test the full pipeline under their own network conditions rather than compare spec sheets. The evidence also points the same way across both categories: **responsiveness and turn-taking now drive perceived quality more than pixels**. Anam's study found responsiveness was the best predictor of user experience. OpenAI's reasoning-heavy voice models lose preference to faster, less "thoughtful" ones. And among human-calling apps, translation and audio AI now separate products whose video specs have converged at 1080p.

The next jump is already visible. Tavus's Griffin, a full-duplex video-to-video model that fooled nearly half of its test participants, suggests that within a year the question will shift from "can people tell it's an AI?" to how AI video calls are disclosed and watermarked. Google already stamps its avatars with SynthID. Commit to platforms on short contracts with that change in mind.
