# Your GPU decides which free changer works

As of November 2026, the best free, high-quality real-time setup is open source: **Deep-Live-Cam** for the face ([GitHub](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)), **w-okada VCClient or its deiteris fork running an RVC voice model** for the voice ([GitHub](https://github.com/w-okada/voice-changer); [GitHub](https://github.com/deiteris/voice-changer)), and **OBS Virtual Camera plus a virtual audio cable** to carry both into the call app ([OBS KB](https://obsproject.com/kb/virtual-camera-guide)). All of these are fully free, with no watermarks, caps or ads. The evidence does not support the full wish, though. **You can have "free" and "very high quality" together, but only on a PC with a dedicated NVIDIA GPU (realistically an RTX 3060 with 8 GB or better).** On AMD or Intel GPUs the same tools run, but slower. On weak laptops and CPU-only machines a live face swap is not practical, and the honest fallback is a 3D avatar plus a lighter voice model. Even on good hardware, expect roughly 15–25 FPS video and 0.2–0.5 seconds of voice delay, not a flawless 30 FPS. The commercial "free tiers" (Swapface, Xpression Camera, Voice.ai, Voicemod) do not get around this: each adds a watermark, a daily cap, rotating voices, or puts live use behind a paywall. Call apps add one more problem. Discord and Google Meet in a browser usually accept virtual devices, but **WhatsApp Desktop often refuses OBS Virtual Camera** and needs a workaround. Use these tools only openly and with consent. Impersonating real people breaks platform rules and, in many places, the law.

## Open-source tools beat every commercial free tier

**For the face, Deep-Live-Cam is the clear free leader.** It has about **97.9k GitHub stars**, an AGPL-3.0 license, needs only **one source photo**, and is still actively developed ([GitHub](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)). Security firm VerSprite tested it against DeepFaceLive, FaceFusion, Roop and SimSwap and called it "the best open-source option" for real time, describing many of the rivals as too slow, uncanny or abandoned ([VerSprite](https://versprite.com/security-resources/real-time-deepfake-face-swapping)). Its useful features for calls include Mouth Mask, which keeps your real mouth movement, and a built-in filter that blocks nude and graphic content. Know its limit: the swap model works at only **128×128 pixels of face resolution**. Sharpness comes from an optional enhancer pass (GFPGAN or CodeFormer), and that pass costs a lot of frame rate (see the next section) ([VerSprite](https://versprite.com/security-resources/real-time-deepfake-face-swapping)). The free version means installing it yourself from GitHub source, which the README warns "requires technical skills". The developer separately sells a prebuilt "Ultimate" edition through deeplivecam.net ([GitHub](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)).

The alternatives each have a catch. **VisoMaster** has richer masking (XSeg occlusion masks, an expression restorer, DeepFaceLab DFM model support) and, unlike Deep-Live-Cam, outputs directly to a virtual camera. But its installer is for "Windows users with an Nvidia GPU" only ([GitHub](https://github.com/visomaster/VisoMaster)). **DeepFaceLive** gives the most realistic results when you use a trained DFM face model, and it has a DirectX12 build that runs on AMD and Intel. But it was **archived on November 13, 2024**, so it gets no more fixes ([GitHub](https://github.com/iperov/DeepFaceLive)). **FaceFusion** (30.6k stars) is popular, but its current README covers only file and batch processing, with no live webcam mode ([GitHub](https://github.com/facefusion/facefusion)). The commercial tools are not truly free for live calls. Swapface's free plan is reported at about 10 swaps a day with a watermark, and several listings say live stream swapping needs a subscription ([ToolMage](https://www.toolmage.com/en/tool/swapface/); [ToolWorthy](https://www.toolworthy.ai/en/tool/swapface)). Xpression Camera's free plan is watermarked ([OpenTools](https://opentools.ai/tools/xpression-camera)), and Facecam offers only a "free version with limited functionality" ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/facecam/about/)).

**For the voice, local RVC models give the most natural results.** The w-okada voice changer (VCClient) has **21.3k stars** and runs RVC v1/v2 and Beatrice voice models in real time ([GitHub](https://github.com/w-okada/voice-changer)). The deiteris fork is RVC-only and tuned for speed: it adds DirectML for AMD and Intel GPUs on Windows and ROCm on Linux ([GitHub](https://github.com/deiteris/voice-changer)). Applio has a Realtime tab that does the same job ([Applio docs mirror](https://mintlify.wiki/IAHispano/Applio/features/realtime)). RVC keeps your own intonation and turns it into another timbre. That sounds far more natural than pitch-shift effects like Clownfish or MorphVOX Junior, which are free but are not AI and sound obviously processed ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-free-ai-voice-changers)). Seed-VC can clone a voice from just 1–30 seconds of audio with no training. But it measured **430 ms total latency on an RTX 3060 Laptop** and was **archived in November 2025** ([GitHub](https://github.com/Plachtaa/seed-vc)). The commercial voice apps again come with limits. Voicemod's free plan has a "very limited daily voice rotation", and its custom-voice tool is Pro-only ([aitoolsatlas](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/voicemod/review)). Voice.ai's free plan is credit-based ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-free-ai-voice-changers)). NVIDIA Broadcast is free, but it only cleans up audio and cannot change your voice ([NVIDIA FAQ](https://www.nvidia.com/en-gb/geforce/broadcasting/broadcast-app/faq)).

## NVIDIA hardware sets the ceiling for live quality

The figures below are the honest core of this report. Even a strong NVIDIA card is not overkill. On an **RTX 4080 Laptop GPU (12 GB)** at 960×540, stock Deep-Live-Cam ran about **15 FPS with the swap alone and about 5 FPS with GFPGAN enhancement**. VerSprite's own optimized fork reached 27 FPS without enhancement and 20 FPS with CodeFormer ([VerSprite](https://versprite.com/security-resources/real-time-deepfake-face-swapping)). The developer's paid build states a minimum of **6 GB of VRAM** and claims 10–20 FPS ([Gumroad](https://hacksider.gumroad.com/l/vccdmm)). A September 2026 setup tutorial concludes that "a discrete NVIDIA GPU with CUDA is the only configuration that holds a comfortable live frame rate". It describes Apple Silicon and AMD/Intel DirectML as usable but slower, and CPU-only as "not practical for live webcam use" ([PromptZone](https://www.promptzone.com/damonwho/deep-live-cam-setup-tutorial-c0c.md)). The voice changer competes for the same GPU. RVC uses about 2 GB of VRAM in FP32, about 1 GB in FP16 ([GitHub](https://github.com/deiteris/voice-changer)). An RTX 3070 running stock w-okada shows about 170–213 ms of chunk latency, and one user reports about 0.5 s total on an RTX 3060 ([rentry guide](https://rentry.co/forkvoicechangerguide)). No one has published a benchmark of face swap and RVC running together on one GPU. A reasonable inference is that **8 GB of VRAM is a tight minimum for both, and 12 GB is comfortable**.

| Hardware tier | Face changer (free) | Voice changer (free) | What to expect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NVIDIA gaming PC (RTX 3060 8 GB+)** | Deep-Live-Cam (`--execution-provider cuda`), or VisoMaster for built-in virtual camera and better masks | w-okada VCClient "cuda" edition or deiteris fork (CUDA), RVC v2 model, RMVPE pitch | Best free result: roughly 15–25 FPS at 540p–720p with enhancer off, voice delay roughly 0.2–0.5 s |
| **AMD / Intel GPU PC (Windows)** | Deep-Live-Cam with `dml` (DirectML) or `openvino` (Intel); DeepFaceLive DX12 build as an archived fallback | deiteris fork with DirectML; RX 6000 / Arc A500 or better recommended | Works, but below CUDA speeds; no published FPS numbers. RX 7000 on DirectML "may be unable" to get under 256 ms voice latency |
| **Weak laptop / CPU-only / integrated graphics** | No practical live face swap. Use a free avatar instead: VSeeFace (Windows) or VTube Studio | Beatrice v2 via VCClient "std" edition (CPU, about 35 MB), or RVC with large chunks (about 448) | Avatar video plus a lighter AI voice; RVC on CPU adds noticeable lag. "Intel integrated GPUs perform poorly" |
| **Mac (Apple Silicon)** | Deep-Live-Cam with `coreml` | VCClient "onnx" or "std" edition (Beatrice/RVC); deiteris macOS build is experimental | Usable but slower than NVIDIA; do not expect smooth 30 FPS. Running both together is unbenchmarked. VCClient Mac app is unsigned |

The tier details come from the Deep-Live-Cam README's execution providers ([GitHub](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)), the deiteris fork's hardware list, which gives a CPU-only floor of an i5-4690K and the "Intel integrated GPUs perform poorly" and RX 7000 warnings ([GitHub](https://github.com/deiteris/voice-changer)), DeepFaceLive's DX12 requirements of an RTX 2070 or RX 5700 XT recommended ([GitHub](https://github.com/iperov/DeepFaceLive)), and VCClient's edition matrix ([GitHub](https://github.com/w-okada/voice-changer)). Beatrice v2 is reported to run single-threaded on CPU in about 35 MB ([Impress Window Forest](https://forest.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/serial/yajiuma/1611613.html)). VSeeFace is free and Windows-only, and its virtual camera can be selected in Discord ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/vseeface/about)). Free cloud GPUs are no escape for weak PCs. Google Colab bans "creating deepfakes" on all runtimes, and on free runtimes it also bans remote desktops and web-UI-driven use ([Colab FAQ](https://research.google.com/colaboratory/faq.html)). Treat any blog claim of "10–15 FPS on CPU" or "60 FPS on a 4090" as unverified, since none of them cites test hardware ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/localfaceswap/deep-live-cam-free-real-time-ai-face-swap-software-1i41)).

## Six steps route face and voice into any call

Deep-Live-Cam does not create a webcam device. Its README tells you to use "a screen capture tool like OBS" ([GitHub](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)). So the video path is Deep-Live-Cam's preview window, captured in OBS, sent out through OBS Virtual Camera, and selected in the call app. The audio path is your microphone, into the voice changer, out to a virtual cable, and then selected as the microphone in the call app.

| Step | What to do | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Download only official builds | Deep-Live-Cam: github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam (or deeplivecam.net). VisoMaster: github.com/visomaster/VisoMaster. Voice: github.com/w-okada/voice-changer, binaries on Hugging Face `wok000/vcclient000`; or github.com/deiteris/voice-changer. OBS: obsproject.com. VB-CABLE: vb-audio.com | [Deep-Live-Cam](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam); [HF wok000](https://huggingface.co/wok000/vcclient000); [OBS KB](https://obsproject.com/kb/virtual-camera-guide) |
| 2. Set up the face swap | Install per the README (Python 3.11–3.14; CUDA 12.8 + cuDNN for NVIDIA). Pick one sharp, front-facing, evenly lit photo. Start "Live", which takes 10–30 s. Keep Face Enhancer and Many Faces off and lower the capture resolution for FPS. Make sure only one onnxruntime package is installed, or it silently falls back to CPU | [GitHub](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam); [PromptZone](https://www.promptzone.com/damonwho/deep-live-cam-setup-tutorial-c0c.md) |
| 3. Route video through OBS | Add a Window Capture of the Deep-Live-Cam preview, crop it, click Start Virtual Camera. Start the virtual camera *before* opening the call app, and on Windows enable "Let desktop apps access your camera" | [OBS KB](https://obsproject.com/kb/video-call-streaming-tutorial); [VDO.Ninja](https://docs.vdo.ninja/common-errors-and-known-issues/obs-virtual-camera-not-working) |
| 4. Set up the voice | Install VB-CABLE (Windows; VB-CABLE or BlackHole on Mac). In VCClient use "server audio" mode: input is your real mic, output is `CABLE Input`. NVIDIA: RMVPE, chunk about 112. CPU: chunk about 448. Raise chunk in steps of 64 until crackling stops. Match everything to 48 kHz | [HF guide](https://huggingface.co/blog/Lenylvt/w-okada); [voicechanger.live](https://voicechanger.live/blog/rvc-troubleshooting-guide); [VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voice-changer-discord-audio-quality-fix) |
| 5. Configure the call app | Camera: "OBS Virtual Camera". Microphone: "CABLE Output". Turn off the app's noise suppression. Wear headphones so echo cancellation can stay off | [Discord community](https://support.discord.com/hc/da/community/posts/360052375433-OBS-Virtual-Cam-to-Discord); [Google Meet Help](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9919960) |
| 6. Resync and test | Clap on camera to compare video and voice delay. Delay whichever is ahead (OBS Render Delay filter on video, or Sync Offset on audio). Test with a friend before a real call | Standard OBS features (inference) |

The call-app settings matter more than they look. Discord's Krisp noise suppression can treat converted voice as noise and muffle it, so set Noise Suppression to None under Voice & Video ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voice-changer-discord-krisp-conflict)). Google Meet's noise cancellation is **on by default for most paid Workspace and Google One 2TB+ accounts**. Turn it off under ⋮ → Settings → Audio. Google itself advises turning it off for electrolarynx users, a sign that it alters synthetic voices ([Google Meet Help](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9919960)). Meet also caches devices, so refresh the page after creating a new virtual mic ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voice-changer-google-meet-guide)). VB-CABLE itself adds under about 14 ms at 48 kHz, so nearly all the voice delay comes from the model ([VB-Audio forum](https://forum.vb-audio.com/viewtopic.php?p=1612)).

## WhatsApp Desktop is the weakest link

Reliability for virtual devices varies by app, and the failures are almost always on the **camera** side, not the microphone. In rough order from most to least reliable: Discord and Meet in a browser, then Telegram Desktop, then WhatsApp Desktop. All evidence here comes from user forums between 2021 and 2025, so behavior may have changed in newer app versions.

**Discord** shows no sign of deliberately blocking virtual cameras, but users report an endless loading spinner after picking OBS Virtual Camera ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/obs-virtual-camera-not-working-with-discord.191384/)). In a July 2025 report, the desktop app ignored the camera while Discord in a web browser worked ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/discord-desktop-app-completely-refusing-to-pick-up-obs-virtual-cam.187674)). **The browser version is the go-to fallback.** **Google Meet** results depend on the browser. The OBS camera has gone missing in Chrome but worked in Edge ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/obs-wont-work-with-google-meet-via-chrome.123878)), and it can look zoomed in on Chrome and Edge but not Firefox ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/virtual-camera-zoomed-in-on-chrome-edge-but-not-on-firefox.174249/)). If one browser fails, try another. **Telegram Desktop** detected OBS Virtual Camera directly in older reports. A newer macOS report (M1, Telegram Desktop 11.3) says OBS stopped appearing as an input for months, with no confirmed fix ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/obs-not-working-on-telegram-dekstop.166901/post-622741)).

**WhatsApp Desktop is where the "works in any call app" promise fails.** Its desktop-calling FAQ has been quoted as saying "virtual audio and video devices aren't supported". The live page could not be loaded to confirm the current wording ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/obs-virtual-cam-is-not-working-with-whatsapp-desktop-app.144629/post-529441)). On Windows, OBS Virtual Camera usually does not appear in the new WhatsApp app. Users explain that OBS registers a DirectShow camera while WhatsApp looks for Media Foundation devices ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/the-obs-virtual-camera-does-not-appear-on-desktop-whatsapp.165969)). The reported fix (December 2024, Windows 11) is the **DroidCam Virtual Output OBS plugin**: install its drivers, restart, and select it in WhatsApp ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/new-whatsapp-desktop-application-does-not-work-with-obs-virtual-camera.140414/post-671686)). The hardware fallback is to send OBS output over HDMI into a USB capture card, which WhatsApp sees as a normal webcam, though cheap cards add uneven latency (same thread). On Mac, the only widely shared fix is stripping WhatsApp's code signature. It does not work on the App Store version, weakens security, and is undone by every update ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/new-whatsapp-desktop-application-does-not-work-with-obs-virtual-camera.140414/post-604608)). A virtual **microphone** is less of a problem: a vendor reports WhatsApp Desktop on Windows accepts one, but nobody has independently tested this ([VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voice-changer-whatsapp-video-desktop)). None of this works inside native phone apps. The practical route is to place the call from the PC version of the account. The other person can still answer on their phone.

## Fake download sites and real people's faces are the risks

The hype around these tools has produced a crop of lookalike sites. Deep-Live-Cam's README names **deeplivecam.net as its only official website** and warns against downloading from anywhere else besides the GitHub repo ([GitHub](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)). Lookalikes are flagged by reputation scanners. Gridinsoft marks **deeplivecamai.com** as suspicious, with a domain only months old as of August 2026 ([Gridinsoft](https://gridinsoft.com/online-virus-scanner/url/deeplivecamai-com)), and describes **deeplive.cam** as hastily made with AI-generated content ([Gridinsoft](https://gridinsoft.com/online-virus-scanner/url/deeplive-cam)). No confirmed malware campaign has been tied to a specific lookalike yet, but fake AI-tool installers are a documented threat. Kaspersky reported the Gipy malware stealing passwords under the guise of an AI application ([Kaspersky](https://www.kaspersky.co.uk/about/press-releases/gipy-malware-steals-passwords-under-the-guise-of-ai-application)). "W-Okada download" pages from software vendors that show up in search results are marketing pages, not the project. Get VCClient only from GitHub or the `wok000` Hugging Face repo ([HF](https://huggingface.co/wok000/vcclient000)). Voice models need care too. Weights.gg, the largest RVC model hub, shut down around **March 31–April 1, 2026**, pushing model sharing to Hugging Face and Discord ([aidirectory](https://aidirectory.com/news/weights-gg-shuts-down-coreweave-weights-and-biases-acquisition-clarification)). Older RVC `.pth` files are Python pickle files that can contain code, so prefer ONNX or safetensors formats where possible. Among commercial apps, Voice.ai drew a 2023 GPL-violation and DRM controversy ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2023/02/08/voiceai_open_source)).

The legal line is simple: transforming yourself, openly, with people who know, is generally fine; becoming someone real without their consent is not. Discord prohibits impersonating real individuals and bans non-consensual intimate media including AI deepfakes, with account removal and law-enforcement referral as penalties ([Discord Guidelines](https://discord.com/guidelines); [Identity explainer](https://discord.com/safety/identity-authenticity-policy-explainer)). Deep-Live-Cam's own README says that if you use a real person's face you must "obtain their consent and clearly label any output as a deepfake" ([GitHub](https://github.com/hacksider/Deep-Live-Cam)). MeanVC2's authors ask for consent from anyone whose voice is converted ([HF](https://huggingface.co/ASLP-lab/MeanVC2)). The bundled VCClient voices carry their own usage terms ([GitHub](https://github.com/w-okada/voice-changer)). The clean choices are fictional faces, consenting friends, licensed bundled voices, or a model trained on your own voice. Never use these tools for identity checks, money requests or romance scenarios. Security firms already use exactly this software to train staff against social-engineering fraud ([VerSprite](https://versprite.com/security-resources/real-time-deepfake-face-swapping)). WhatsApp, Telegram and Google Meet impersonation clauses and specific national deepfake laws were not checked against primary sources for this report.

## Conclusion

The real bottleneck is no longer software cost or quality. It is hardware and plumbing. The best tools are already free, and the difference between a convincing call and a stuttering one comes down to having CUDA and to whether the call app accepts a virtual device. That turns the usual buying decision around: someone on a weak laptop gets more from a free VTuber avatar and a CPU Beatrice voice than from paying for a commercial "live" tier that still depends on the same local compute or cloud round-trips. For WhatsApp in particular, the dependable route is to move the call to Discord or Meet in a browser rather than fight driver workarounds.

Two trends are worth watching. First, the most realistic legacy tools (DeepFaceLive, Seed-VC) are archived, so quality now depends on a few actively maintained projects, which means fewer choices and a bigger target for lookalike sites. Second, streaming voice research is pushing latency down on CPU: MeanVC2 reports about 110 ms on a single CPU core ([HF](https://huggingface.co/ASLP-lab/MeanVC2)) and LLVC under 20 ms ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.00873v1)). Neither is a consumer app yet. If either reaches a packaged tool, the voice half of "any PC" becomes achievable well before the face half does, since face swapping is still held back by GPU requirements and 128-pixel models.
